# Chapter 20: Electric Forces and Electric Fields

## Key Concepts/Topics

### 1. Electric Charge
- Two types: positive (+) and negative (-)
- **Conservation of Charge**: Net charge in an isolated system remains constant
- **Quantization of Charge**: Charge exists in discrete multiples of elementary charge e (e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C)
- ** Fundamental charge**: e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C (proton +, electron -)
- Like charges repel, opposite charges attract

### 2. Electric Forces (Coulomb's Law)
- Force between two point charges
- Force is along the line joining the charges
- Like charges repel, opposite attract
- **Coulomb's constant**: k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²

### 3. Electric Fields
- Field exists around any charged object
- **Vector field** - has both magnitude and direction
- Direction: away from positive, toward negative
- Field lines show direction and strength (denser = stronger)
- Superposition principle applies

### 4. Conductors and Insulators
- **Conductors**: Allow charge to flow freely (metals)
- **Insulators**: Charge cannot flow (rubber, glass, plastic)
- **Semiconductors**: Conductivity between conductors and insulators
- **Induction**: Charge separation without direct contact

### 5. Electric Potential Energy & Potential (Voltage)
- Work done to move a charge in an electric field
- Scalar quantity (no direction)
- Related to potential energy per unit charge

### 6. Millikan Oil Drop Experiment
- Determined the elementary charge e
- Showed charge is quantized

### 7. Photoelectric Effect
- Light ejects electrons from metal
- Threshold frequency depends on metal
- Evidence for particle nature of light (photons)

# Chapter 21: Electric Circuits

## Key Concepts/Topics

### 1. Electric Current
- Flow of electric charge
- **Conventional current**: Flows from + to - (opposite to electron flow)
- **Drift velocity**: Average velocity of charge carriers
- **Current**: I = q/t (charge per unit time)
- **Ampere**: 1 A = 1 C/s

### 2. Voltage (Electric Potential Difference)
- Potential difference that causes current to flow
- Source: battery, generator, etc.
- **EMF**: Electromotive force (energy per charge provided by source)

### 3. Resistance
- Opposition to current flow
- Depends on: length, cross-sectional area, material, temperature
- **Resistivity (ρ)**: Material property
- **Temperature coefficient**: R increases with temperature for metals

### 4. Ohm's Law
- V = IR
- **Ohmic materials**: V ∝ I (linear)
- **Non-ohmic**: Not linear (diodes, bulbs)

### 5. Power in Electric Circuits
- Rate of energy transfer
- P = IV = I²R = V²/R

### 6. Series Circuits
- Single path for current
- **Same current** through all components
- Voltages add up
- Total resistance: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + ...

### 7. Parallel Circuits
- Multiple paths for current
- **Same voltage** across all branches
- Currents add up
- Total resistance: 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ...

### 8. Combination Circuits
- Mix of series and parallel
- Simplify step by step

### 9. Kirchhoff's Rules
- **Junction Rule**: Current in = current out (conservation of charge)
- **Loop Rule**: Sum of potential changes around loop = 0 (conservation of energy)

### 10. Capacitors in Circuits
- Store charge
- Q = CV
- Capacitors in series: 1/C = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂
- Capacitors in parallel: C = C₁ + C₂

### 11. RC Circuits
- Resistor-capacitor circuits
- Time constant: τ = RC
